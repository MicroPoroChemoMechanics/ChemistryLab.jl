Friedel's and Kuzel's salts from 0 to 99 °C ​

Before this page

CEM I 52.5 N HTS and CEM II/A-L 42.5 R from 0 to 60 °C, the same comparison on two cements without chloride.

Balonis (2019) calculated four model mixtures of tricalcium aluminate, portlandite and water with sulfate, chloride and, in two of them, calcite, from 0 to 99 °C, and stated where each chloride AFm phase gives way to monosulfate. Her Friedel's and Kuzel's salts are the records of Cemdata18 (Lothenbach et al., 2019) (her Table 1); she mixes Friedel's salt ideally with the hydroxy-AFm and with monocarbonate, which Cemdata18 does not. This page computes the same mixtures with Cemdata18 as the package ships it, Friedel's salt pure, and compares.

1. The mixtures ​

Each holds 0.01 mol of C₃A, 0.015 mol of portlandite and 60 ml of water, the sulfate as calcium sulfate at SO₃/Al₂O₃ = 1, the chloride as CaCl₂ at the ratio Balonis (2019) writes 2Cl/Al₂O₃ (1 for Friedel's salt), and the carbonate as calcite (data/literature/Balonis2019.json). ASSUMED: the 60 ml of water are 60 g; the activity model is the package's extended Debye–Hückel equation for a KOH solution, where the article uses Truesdell and Jones' with individual ion sizes.

julia
using ChemistryLab, DynamicQuantities, OptimaSolver, Printf
include(joinpath(pkgdir(ChemistryLab), "scripts", "balonis2019_temperature.jl"))

cs = b19_system()
mixtures = literature_table(B19, "temperature_scans")
for (fig, cl, co2) in zip(mixtures.figure, mixtures.cl2_al2o3, mixtures.co2_al2o3)
    @printf("%-8s 2Cl/Al2O3 = %.1f, CO2/Al2O3 = %.2f\n", fig, ustrip(cl), ustrip(co2))
end

2. The phases from 0 to 99 °C ​

Each mixture every five degrees from 0 to 95 °C and at 99 °C, the volume of each phase in cm³:

julia
scans = Dict{String, Any}()
for fig in mixtures.figure
    scans[fig] = b19_scan(cs, fig)
end
shown = ("Kuzel's salt", "Friedel's salt", "ettringite", "monosulfate", "monocarbonate", "portlandite", "total")

    println(fig, ", cm³")

3. Where each phase gives way ​

The temperatures Balonis (2019) states, against the two temperatures of the scan between which the calculation loses (or forms) the phase (b19_transition):

julia
tr = literature_table(B19, "transitions")
println("figure   phase            event   article   computed")
for (fig, phase, event, T) in zip(tr.figure, tr.phase, tr.event, tr.temperature_C)
    c = b19_transition(scans[fig], phase, event)
    computed = c === nothing ? "never" : c[2] === nothing ? "still at $(c[1]) °C" :
        c[1] === nothing ? "already at $(c[2]) °C" : "between $(c[1]) and $(c[2]) °C"
    @printf("%-8s %-16s %-6s %5.0f °C   %s\n", fig, phase, event, ustrip(T), computed)
end

4. Why Kuzel's salt stays ​

Kuzel's salt is half monosulfate and half Friedel's salt, and one water: C4AsClH12 = ½ monosulphate12 + ½ C4AClH10 + H2O. The Gibbs energy of that reaction, from the four records, says at which temperature the two AFm phases would take over from it, pure:

julia
g(s, T) = ustrip(us"kJ/mol", B19_DB[s][:ΔₐG⁰](T = (T + 273.15) * u"K", P = 1.0e5u"Pa"; unit = true))
ΔrG(T) = g("monosulphate12", T) / 2 + g("C4AClH10", T) / 2 + g("H2O@", T) - g("C4AsClH12", T)
for T in (0, 25, 55, 85, 99)
    @printf("%3d °C  ΔrG = %+.2f kJ/mol\n", T, ΔrG(T))
end
  0 °C  ΔrG = +1.83 kJ/mol
 25 °C  ΔrG = +1.59 kJ/mol
 55 °C  ΔrG = +1.19 kJ/mol
 85 °C  ΔrG = +0.65 kJ/mol
 99 °C  ΔrG = +0.34 kJ/mol

5. What the comparison says ​

Where chloride is plentiful, the calculation follows Balonis (2019). With 2Cl/Al₂O₃ = 1, Friedel's salt goes between 90 and 95 °C, where the article states 80 and about 90 °C, ettringite with it in the carbonate mixture, and the solids have lost 22.8 and 26.0 % of their volume at 99 °C, against the article's 23 and 25 %. The monocarbonate of the carbonate mixture with less chloride goes between 50 and 55 °C, where the article states about 50.

Where it is scarce, Kuzel's salt takes the place of the phases of Balonis (2019). With 2Cl/Al₂O₃ = 0.5 the calculation keeps Kuzel's salt up to 99 °C and loses Friedel's salt by 25 °C (by 45 °C with carbonate), where the article keeps Kuzel's salt up to 28 °C only and Friedel's salt up to 70 °C; its monosulfate appears between 50 and 55 °C instead of about 28. With 2Cl/Al₂O₃ = 1 Kuzel's salt forms above 55 °C, where the article forms monosulfate from 60 °C. Of the same records, the reaction of Section 4 costs Kuzel's salt 1.6 kJ/mol at 25 °C and 0.3 at 99 °C, never zero below 100 °C, so pure phases keep it. The article's Friedel's salt and hydroxy-AFm are solid solutions, which lower the competing AFm phases by about as much: the ideal mixing of two members at equal fractions is worth RT ln 2, 1.7 kJ/mol at 25 °C. The package does not declare those solutions; a calculation that needs the article's Kuzel's salt must declare them in its system.
