Inside a host code: equilibria and kinetics at every node ​

Before this page

Solving an equilibrium and Writing a kinetic model. The figures below come from three scripts of scripts/bench_coupling/, named in Reproducing the figures.

A transport or a structural code, such as a finite-volume or finite-element solver of a porous medium, calls the chemistry at each of its nodes (a Gauss point, a cell) at every one of its steps. Between two calls it changes the element budget b of the node, or it advances the time by its own step Δt. The chemistry answers with the composition at that budget, or after that step, and the host reads from it what its own equations need: a porosity, a pH, the amount of a solid, a flux.

The cost of the coupled computation is then

cost = (number of nodes) × (number of host steps) × (cost of one call)

and only the last factor is the chemistry's. This page measures it for each way of calling, and says which call to make when.

Equilibrium at a node ​

The entry points, and what each one costs ​

A node whose budget changed by a little between two calls is the common case, and the one this section measures: a Portland paste (CEM I 52.5 N, Bogue, w/b = 0.5), with and without the C-S-H surface of Guo et al. The calls, from the most to the least general:

  • cold: certified is equilibrate_certified on the state given: it solves from every back end that is loaded and returns the answer its certificate proves.

  • cold: one back end is solve of an EquilibriumSolver built once for the system: OptimaSolver's answer, polished by the dual Newton method. cold: solver rebuilt builds that solver at every call.

  • warm is solve_certified of a DualEquilibriumSolver built once, started from the node's previous answer: the dual Newton alone.

  • warm + multipliers resumes it also from the multipliers of that answer, y0, which a dual_state reference returns with every solve.

  • warm + multipliers - report skips what the certificate covers (scope, the curvature of the answer, the range of the activity model) with report = false, for a caller that reads only optimal and the residuals.

What decides the cost is where the solve starts. Building the solver is not: the times with the solver rebuilt at every call are those with it built once. From the node's previous answer and its multipliers, a call takes under a third of the time of a cold one on the paste and half of it with the surface, and allocates five and two and a half times less; report = false takes a further 5 to 15 % off.

What to do at a node ​

A node keeps its last answer and the multipliers of that answer, and each thread uses a solver of its own, since a solver keeps the start of its next solve. Below, four nodes of a column of calcite receive carbon dioxide at every step of their host, each more than the one before it; each call is the warm one, with the certified cold route as the fallback of an answer the certificate does not prove.

julia
using DynamicQuantities, OptimaSolver

sp = Dict(symbol(x) => x for x in build_species(datapath("slop98-inorganic-thermofun.json"); verbose = false))
cs = ChemicalSystem([sp[x] for x in split("H2O@ H+ OH- CO2@ HCO3- CO3-2 Ca+2 Cal")],
                    ["H2O@", "H+", "Ca+2", "CO3-2", "Zz"])
model = DiluteSolutionModel()
st0 = ChemicalState(cs)
set_quantity!(st0, "Cal", 0.05u"mol")
set_quantity!(st0, "H2O@", 1.0u"kg")
set_quantity!(st0, "H+", 1.0e-7u"mol")
set_quantity!(st0, "OH-", 1.0e-7u"mol")

des = DualEquilibriumSolver(cs, model)               # once
first_answer, _ = equilibrate_certified(st0; model)  # the nodes start equal: solved once
states = fill(first_answer, 4)                       # the last answer of each node
ys = Vector{Union{Nothing, Vector{Float64}}}(nothing, 4)    # and its multipliers
co2 = des.A[:, findfirst(x -> symbol(x) == "CO2@", cs.species)]   # the budget of 1 mol of CO₂

fallbacks = 0
for step in 1:5, k in eachindex(states)
    b = des.A * [ustrip(us"mol", x) for x in states[k].n] .+ 1.0e-4 * k .* co2
    y = Ref{Any}(nothing)
    st, cert = solve_certified(des, (states[k],); b, y0 = ys[k], dual_state = y, report = false)
    if cert.optimal
        ys[k] = y[].y
    else                                             # the cold route, where the warm one failed
        global fallbacks += 1
        st, cert = equilibrate_certified(states[k]; model, b)
        ys[k] = nothing
    end
    states[k] = st
end
@printf("fallbacks: %d of 20 calls; calcite left: %s mol\n", fallbacks,
        join((@sprintf("%.5f", ustrip(us"mol", moles(st, "Cal"))) for st in states), ", "))
fallbacks: 0 of 20 calls; calcite left: 0.04951, 0.04909, 0.04874, 0.04846 mol

Two things a call should not do:

  • start from the host's initial state at every step: the cold route then solves again what the previous step already solved;

  • solve two identical nodes twice: nodes that share a budget, at the start of a computation or where the transport has not yet arrived, share their answer, as the four nodes above share their first one.

A derivative with respect to b, which an implicit host needs for its Jacobian, comes from the conditions of equilibrium at the answer (Differentiating an equilibrium): one factorization serves every partial derivative, and no second equilibrium is solved.

Kinetics at a node ​

A kinetic call advances the node over the host's step Δt. Its cost is the number of equilibria it solves times the cost of one, and which of the two dominates is decided by the rate laws, before any choice of integrator:

  • laws that read only the kinetic amounts (Parrott–Killoh, an imposed stoichiometry): the equilibrium partition, when there is one, is solved once per accepted step and held within it (speciation = :frozen, which integrate takes by itself, When the partition may be frozen within a step); the problem is mildly stiff, and the integrator that takes the fewest steps wins;

  • laws that read the solution (a saturation ratio, a pH): the partition is solved at every evaluation of the right-hand side, and the relaxation to equilibrium makes the problem stiff: an explicit integrator is held to steps of the order of its relaxation time.

Integrators and tolerances ​

Three cases, integrated over their interval by each method that using OrdinaryDiffEq exports, at reltol = 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁶, 10⁻⁸ and abstol = 10⁻³ reltol mol; the deviation is the largest one from a Rodas5P run at 10⁻¹⁰, relative to the largest magnitude of each component:

  • clinker: the four Parrott–Killoh laws of Hydration kinetics of a CEM I 52.5 R clinker, products imposed, semi-adiabatic cell, no equilibrium partition, 7 days;

  • clinker_eq: the same clinker, products left to thermodynamics, isothermal, 2 days: one equilibrium per accepted step;

  • calcite: calcite dissolving under r = k(1 − Ω), as in Coupling kinetics and equilibrium, 10⁵ s: one equilibrium per evaluation of the right-hand side.

What the table says, case by case:

  • without a partition, or with one solved per accepted step, the explicit Tsit5 is the fastest at every tolerance, five to six times faster than Rodas5P without a partition and 1.5 to 2 times with one: the laws are not stiff enough for a stiff method to pay for its Jacobian, and with a partition Tsit5 takes fewer steps, hence fewer equilibria; at 10⁻⁸ the stiff methods are the more accurate;

  • with the partition read by the law, Tsit5 takes tens of thousands of steps, bounded by stability rather than accuracy, and three orders of magnitude more time; AutoTsit5(Rosenbrock23()), which starts explicit and switches when it detects stiffness, is the fastest there, and Rodas5P close behind;

  • the deviation follows reltol, within a factor of sixty for the low-order methods at the loosest tolerance, so the tolerance is set by the accuracy the host needs; tightening it by two orders costs Rodas5P about twice the time, and the second-order Rosenbrock23 about four times.

So, for a call the host makes at every node and every step:

julia
using OrdinaryDiffEq

# laws that read only the kinetic amounts
ks = KineticsSolver(; ode_solver = Tsit5(), reltol = 1.0e-4, abstol = 1.0e-7)
# laws that read the solution
ks = KineticsSolver(; ode_solver = AutoTsit5(Rosenbrock23()), reltol = 1.0e-4, abstol = 1.0e-7)

abstol is in moles: scale it on the amounts of the node, 10⁻³ reltol times its largest kinetic amount, so that it means the same on a cubic millimeter of paste as on a kilogram. Rodas5P, the default of integrate, is the safe choice when the stiffness is not known: on these cases it is never more than six times slower than the fastest.

A step imposed by the host ​

A host code does not hand the chemistry an interval to integrate: it imposes its own steps, one call per step and per node, each from the state the last one ended at. Two ways to take such a step:

  • an ODE restarted at every step: a KineticsProblem over the step, from the state the last step ended at, integrate, and the speciated state at the end of the step, speciated_states;

  • one implicit step per host step, kinetic_step: one Gibbs minimization with the extents among its unknowns, first order in the step (The implicit step).

Two keywords make a restarted run follow the uninterrupted one, at a cost that does not grow with the length of the history:

Below, the calcite of the nodes above dissolves under a first-order law in its degree of reaction, r = k n/n_initial, over ten host steps of 1000 s, by a run restarted at each step and by one uninterrupted run:

julia
using OrderedCollections, OrdinaryDiffEq

dissolution = Reaction(OrderedDict(cs["Cal"] => 1.0), OrderedDict(cs["Ca+2"] => 1.0, cs["CO3-2"] => 1.0);
                       symbol = "calcite")
dissolution[:rate] = KineticFunc((T, P, t, n, lna, n0) -> 2.0e-6 * n["Cal"] / n0["Cal"], NamedTuple(), u"mol/s")
es = EquilibriumSolver(cs, model, OptimaOptimizer())
ks = KineticsSolver(; ode_solver = Rodas5P(), reltol = 1.0e-6, abstol = 1.0e-12)
problem(st, span; n_initial = nothing) =
    KineticsProblem(cs, [dissolution], st, span; activity_model = model, equilibrium_solver = es, n_initial)

state = first_answer                              # the history starts here
for k in 1:10
    span = (1000.0 * (k - 1), 1000.0 * k)
    kp = problem(state, span; n_initial = first_answer)
    global state = only(speciated_states(integrate(kp, ks), kp; times = [span[2]], start = state))
end
whole = problem(first_answer, (0.0, 1.0e4))
uninterrupted = only(speciated_states(integrate(whole, ks), whole; times = [1.0e4]))
@printf("calcite after 10⁴ s: %.8f mol restarted, %.8f mol uninterrupted\n",
        ustrip(us"mol", moles(state, "Cal")), ustrip(us"mol", moles(uninterrupted, "Cal")))
calcite after 10⁴ s: 0.03341243 mol restarted, 0.03341243 mol uninterrupted

  • n_initial = state0 is what the rate laws read as their n_initial, the amounts the history started from. Without it, each run counts from the start of its own step, and a Parrott–Killoh law, whose degree of hydration is 1 − n/n_initial, restarts at α = 0 at every step. kinetic_step takes the same keyword.

  • start = state hands the replay of the speciated state the answer the last step ended with; without it, the replay seeds a chain of certified solves from the start of the run.

The implicit step is eight to twenty times cheaper than a restarted ODE, and its deviation is that of a first-order method over the host's step, of the order of 10⁻² on the clinker at one-hour steps, where the restarted ODE meets its tolerance; on calcite, whose relaxation is far shorter than a host step, both reach the equilibrium. A restarted ODE pays at every host step what an uninterrupted run pays once, a start from a small step and the speciated state at its end, so that a single run over the whole history of the clinker costs less than one of its host steps; on the clinker the stiff Rodas5P is then the cheapest, since the restart takes away the long steps the explicit methods win with.

So, when the host imposes its steps:

  • the implicit step when the host step is short against the time over which the rates change, or when its accuracy is that of the host's own time discretization, first order as well; kinetic_step_adaptive when it is not, which halves the step until its error estimate is within tolerance and the step is certified;

  • a restarted ODE, with n_initial and start, when the trajectory within a host step must be resolved to a tolerance.
